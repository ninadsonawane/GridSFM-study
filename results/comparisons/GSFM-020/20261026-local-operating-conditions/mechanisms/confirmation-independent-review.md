# Independent confirmation review

The six confirmation inputs have accepted AC references and pass the DC formulation's own constraints in the saved report. Independent recomputation from all 52 native states using direct pi-branch equations agrees with the saved P/Q mean, p95 and maximum to at most 4.73e-11 MW/MVAr. Every native state fails power balance under the frozen 0.001 MW/MVAr criterion. No model inference, solver call, state repair or changed threshold was used in this review. Machine-readable checks are in `confirmation-independent-review.json`; the reproducible script is `experiments/gsfm020/review_confirmations.py`.

Three strongest supported findings:

1. **The component controls separate the local voltage response to P and Q.** Starting at P=Q=0.93, increasing only P to 0.935 changes v2 bus 235 from its 0.94 pu lower clamp to 0.978631 pu; increasing only Q to 0.935 leaves it at the lower clamp, with the pre-clamp value decreasing from 0.938553 to 0.927614 pu. In the Q-only confirmation, accepted AC total Qg rises 41.333691 MVAr while v1.1 and v2 native totals fall 0.824368 and 2.044310 MVAr. Actual Qg-clipped row memberships are unchanged, and their returned Qg values do not move, so that aggregate decline comes from the other generator rows. DC's four saved arrays are exactly unchanged for the Q-only perturbation, consistent with its formulation. This identifies a local directional response; it does not establish a universal derivative or a beneficial correction.

2. **A fixed-total redistribution produces a large nonlocal v2 change.** At confirmation strengths 0.75 and 1.25, total Pd/Qd agree to floating-point summation precision, and demand at buses 235 and 238 is exactly unchanged. V2 bus 235 changes from the upper clamp (pre-clamp 1.072565, native 1.06 pu) to the lower clamp (pre-clamp 0.788651, native 0.94 pu). Qmax rises from 7481.574 to 21387.520 MVAr and Qmean from 385.755 to 531.265 MVAr. This is a bracket between sampled conditions, not an exact threshold. Counterevidence matters: Qmax at 0.75 is lower than at 0.5, and it decreases slightly again from 1.25 to 1.5; neither severity nor the response is globally monotone. Approximation errors and residual statistics must remain separate.

3. **The existing finite-rated ties reveal a release- and direction-dependent native violation.** At a +4% area offset, v1.1 predicts 626.934 MVA on each existing 600 MVA tie, while v2 predicts 495.762 MVA; the accepted AC result is 164.710 MVA. At −4%, v1.1/v2 predict 379.838/435.210 MVA and accepted AC again 164.710 MVA. This confirms a v1.1 tie violation at one accepted-reference operating point and an asymmetry in native responses. It does not make v2 AC feasible. DC's 470.517 MW passes its active-power constraint, which is distinct from apparent-power feasibility.

Limits to retain in the report: all 52 native outputs remain infeasible; case300 is below the Microsoft documented 500-bus minimum; these are two fixed structures with original ordering, not a distribution-level generalization claim. The ±7.5% area cases have UNKNOWN AC references after the allowed attempts, so their native violations can be scored but reference accuracy cannot be ranked and system infeasibility cannot be inferred. The representation diagnostic establishes large preprocessing dependence; its interventions are controls, not new electrical operating points. The newer adapted framework and the frozen base checkpoint have different scopes. No novelty claim follows from these results alone.

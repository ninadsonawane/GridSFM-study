# Frozen GridSFM: confirmed operating-condition findings

**Completed locally on 26 October 2026.** Twenty discovery inputs and six preregistered confirmation inputs were evaluated with all four methods. AC-OPF supplied 24 independently accepted references; two attempted references remain UNKNOWN. All 26 DC points passed their own equations. **Neither frozen model produced a jointly AC-feasible native state: 0/26 for v1.1 and 0/26 for base v2.** All 929 previous tracked study files were preserved.

The strongest result is sensitivity to the *location and composition* of demand, with distinctly different responses from the two releases. A lower dispatch error or a lower maximum imbalance did not establish physical validity.

## Three strongest findings

**1. V2 has a confirmed nonlocal voltage transition; large aggregate improvements can be concentrated at one branch.** At fixed total demand, relocation strength +0.75 → +1.25 changes native Vm235 from the upper bound 1.06 to the lower bound 0.94 pu. Its pre-clamp values are 1.072565 → 0.788651; native Vm238 remains 1.06. The verified AC Vm235 changes by only 0.0000543 pu. Neither bus 235 nor238 has its demand altered.

On original branch 321, connecting235–238 with x=0.0006 pu, reactive flow at238 changes −365.21 → 18,710.07 MVAr; the AC value changes only 59.479 → 59.446. The small branch reactance amplifies the model's voltage mismatch. An exact branch-equation decomposition attributes +19,079.990 MVAr to the voltage-gap term and −4.709 MVAr to the angle terms, summing to the observed +19,075.281 MVAr change; the charging term is unchanged. The saved signed-balance decomposition locates the resulting residual, rather than attributing it to total demand. Of the 43,653.08 MVAr increase in summed absolute Q imbalance, 35,646.77 occurs at235/238 and 8,006.31 at the other 298 buses. There are 180 worsening buses and 120 improving buses. Almost all of the 13.52 MW increase in active-flow MAE comes from native prediction movement: the symmetric attribution is +13.490 MW native and +0.028 MW reference. This is an algebraic split of error movement, not causal identification. V1.1 does not show the same surge.

Discovery strengths +0.5/+1.5 and the two held-out strengths support this local transition. **They do not show a universal monotonic trend or identify an exact threshold:** +0.75 is still upper-clipped and has a lower Qmax than +0.5; reverse-direction redistributions do not show the same surge. Strength 1 relocates 63.82 MW and 16.02 MVAr between the historically fixed two five-bus groups. Their admittance-based selection is a source-data proxy, not a voltage-stability classification.

The global-load experiment exposes the converse effect. From 0.9301 to 0.945, v2 Qmax falls 61.39%, but Q p95 rises 13.79%; the other 298 buses collectively worsen by 1,842.61 MVAr while 235/238 account for more than the total improvement. The first sampled lower-clamp exit lies between 0.9301 and 0.9303. The held-out P-only control reproduces the exit; Q-only does not. Lowering one large hotspot is therefore insufficient evidence of improved operating validity.

**2. Both releases fail to track the verified reactive-generation response.** With P fixed at 0.93, increasing Q from 0.93 to 0.94 to 0.95 raises AC total Qg from 5,918.79 to 6,001.48 to 6,084.30 MVAr. Native v1.1 instead gives 2,388.32 → 2,386.66 → 2,385.00; v2 gives 2,741.41 → 2,737.43 → 2,733.84. The held-out Q=0.935 case confirms the opposite directions: AC +41.334 MVAr, v1.1 −0.824 and v2 −2.044. Exactly the same 7 and 26 Qg rows remain clipped; their total change is zero. The decrease comes from unclipped outputs. DC's exact invariance supplies the expected control. This is a measured directional failure at these settings, not a universal law that every AC-OPF solution must respond monotonically.

**3. V1.1 has a confirmed directional tie overload that v2 avoids at the same conditions.** The existing duplicate-area network scales area-A demand by 1+δ and area-B by 1−δ, preserving totals. At δ=+0.04, v1.1 overloads both ties by 26.934 MVA; at−0.04 it stays below their limits. Both independently accepted AC references load the ties to only 164.710 MVA. V2 stays below 600 MVA in both directions, although its balances remain invalid. The ±0.025 and±0.075 discovery points bracket the sampled transition; both releases violate both ties at±0.075. Those latter AC attempts remain UNKNOWN. The duplicate-area symmetry makes the direction difference informative, but the bounded study cannot separate learned behavior from positional representation effects.

## Feasibility, accuracy and diagnostic limits

All 52 native model states violate P/Q balance. No native generator or voltage-limit excess exceeds the fixed acceptance tolerances; recorded angle-limit violations are zero. Passing these individual limits after shipped clipping is not joint feasibility. Actual pre/post clipping events and locations are saved separately from values merely near a bound. Original case300 thermal assessment is N/A; the connected network's 822 original branches remain unrated. DC Qg, Vm and joint AC feasibility are N/A.

Across the 24 accepted-reference inputs, v2 has lower Pg MAE than v1.1 in 24/24, but higher Qg, Vm and active-flow MAE in 24/24. Aligned-angle MAE is lower in 14/24. These are paired results on this selected study set, not a general release ranking. P/Q mean, median, p95, maximum, violation counts, severity bands and locations, plus all five error families, are retained in the full metrics.

The bounded representation diagnostic used 19 actual neural forwards and 21 worker invocations; two Windows cache-path failures occurred before inference and are preserved. Repeat, poisoned-label and independent-process returns were bit-identical within this local environment. Fixed-order input/output mapping and independent physics passed. Reversing physically equivalent branch order nevertheless changed maximum Pg by 247.12 MW in v2 and 125.19 MW in v1.1. Restoring mapped cycle features reduced the v2 difference to 0.000358 MW; v1.1 required both cycle and transformer-feature substitutions to reach 0.000429 MW. V1.1 bus-order mediation remains incomplete. These altered-feature outputs are diagnostics, not replacements for shipped predictions. All operating comparisons therefore retain original ordering; no permutation-invariance or general robustness claim is justified. See the [diagnostic review](../../results/comparisons/GSFM-020/20261026-local-operating-conditions/diagnostic/diagnostic-review.md).

## What the evidence supports

Expected physics includes DC's Q-demand invariance, the verified AC reactive response at these settings, and amplification of voltage error by a low-reactance branch. Generic accuracy/physics disagreement and voltage-error amplification already appear in [Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05959v1). The later [GridSFM adaptation/Newton workflow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30173v2) has a different evaluation boundary from the frozen base checkpoint evaluated here. Original case300 is below the [v1.1 documented 500-bus size boundary](https://github.com/microsoft/GridSFM/blob/1ca775fd436d7ce013a1c0ab946e61ac7ef59ad6/model/README.md); duplicating it to 600 buses does not establish training-distribution similarity.

**Proposed central claim:** Under fixed original representation on case300 and its existing two-area extension, the released frozen GridSFM checkpoints respond differently to demand composition, location and transfer direction. Nearby preregistered controls confirm a localized v2 voltage/imbalance transition, an opposite reactive-generation response in both releases, and a v1.1 directional tie overload. Conventional references remain feasible for the confirmed cases, but no native jointly feasible model state was found.

This is reproducible, release-specific empirical evidence worth developing further. **Novelty and publication readiness are not established.** The scope is two related networks, selected conditions, frozen native outputs and the disclosed finite limits. No training, checkpoint selection, PF completion, OPF repair, publication or correspondence occurred.

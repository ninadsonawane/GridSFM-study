## Exactly three candidate angles

| Angle | Falsifiable question and required evidence | Closest precedent | Feasibility/risk | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen robustness | Does held-out area-load imbalance or a single-tie outage increase frozen-model tail errors and all-constraint failures? A fixed manifest, matched numerical reference and independent physics evaluator suffice. | GridSFM zero-shot evaluations; OPFData topology variants | Lowest integration burden; novelty is narrow and depends on discovering a reproducible failure pattern. Pretraining overlap may remain unknown. | Deadline fallback and baseline in every variant. |
| Matched parameter-efficient adaptation | At one fixed target-data budget, how much of full FT's improvement does FFN-LoRA recover under held-out area-transfer stress? Same released checkpoint, labels, splits, losses, validation budget and evaluation; independent seeds. | GridSFM full FT; Karim et al. PF adaptation; PowerGraph-LLM OPF LoRA | Uses Ninad's archived implementation, but custom dataset adapter and checkpoint compatibility are real integration risks. | **Primary recommendation**, conditional on passing the conversion and short-training gate. |
| Learned solver initialization | Does the predicted initialization reduce complete time-to-feasible-AC-OPF under the same tolerance and failure/retry policy? | GridSFM May/October experiments; Smart-PGSim | Additional solver hooks, repeated timing, warm/cold policy and numerical variability. A 600-bus solver may already be fast. | Optional later study, not an additional required contribution. |

Recommended question: **On one constructed two-area case300 system, can FFN-LoRA recover the accuracy and physical-feasibility gains of full GridSFM fine-tuning under held-out loading and inter-area transfer, using a matched small target-data budget?** A single-tie outage is a deliberately withheld stress slice. This is operating-regime and one contingency-family evidence, not evidence of generalization to arbitrary unseen interconnections.

The defensible contribution is a controlled empirical comparison with transparent conversion and per-circuit physics checks. It is not a new LoRA algorithm, a first power-grid foundation model, an official IEEE 600-bus benchmark, or a global-feasibility certificate. The literature search supports a plausible narrow gap; it is not an exhaustive proof of novelty or an acceptance prediction.

Proposed hypotheses, to preregister before opening test results:

1. Adaptation reduces median cost error and tail nodal residuals relative to the frozen checkpoint on held-out area-load shifts. Failure to improve either is informative.
2. At a fixed label budget, the validation-selected FFN-LoRA configuration recovers a substantial share of full FT's improvement while updating fewer weights. Report the measured trade-off; do not assume parity.
3. Improvements on intact loading scenarios do not necessarily transfer to the withheld single-tie-outage slice. Report that slice separately, including failures.

For a stronger non-inferiority claim, agree numerical margins with the professor *before* final test evaluation; otherwise use effect sizes and uncertainty, not a post-hoc equivalence declaration. If the dataset adapter cannot pass one-scenario label/feature round-trip and a short backward-pass pilot by the agreed integration deadline, switch the title/question to frozen robustness and remove adaptation claims.

## Version and interpretation corrections

The October paper explicitly identifies the May whitepaper as a preliminary, non-peer-reviewed version and says the newer work changes the backbone and fine-tuning design (Introduction footnote). Treat them as separate citations. Its abstract attributes 2.45% zero-shot error to a 10,000-bus case, while Section VI-B describes held-out operating points on the 54 pretraining grids. Avoid reusing that ambiguous headline; identify the experiment and table instead. The local projection theorem is conditional on neighborhood/regularity assumptions, not an unconditional certificate for arbitrary model outputs. [October paper, Introduction, V–VI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.30173v2).

No inspected source establishes that the exact official MATPOWER case300, its PGLib derivative, or the proposed interconnected case appeared in the pinned checkpoint's training corpus. Absence of `case300` from the October paper text is not a complete contamination audit. Require a checkpoint-specific training-topology manifest or author confirmation before calling the target demonstrably unseen. A 600-bus count alone does not establish distributional similarity.

The archived 6,470-bus LoRA case study remains historical evidence. Its [29 September audit in research-learning](https://github.com/ninadsonawane/research-learning/tree/main/output/whitepaper/release-audit-2026-09-29) explicitly reports unavailable raw run artifacts, unmatched published full FT, and no independent retraining. Nothing in this research package turns those observations into new case300/600-bus numerical results.

## Reading and verification limits

This review inspected extracted full-paper text and relevant tables. Browser PDF screenshot calls were unavailable; no visual-table QA is claimed. No training or solver experiment was run for this literature review. Publication status uses official author/institution/conference records where available; arXiv entries without a verified venue remain labeled preprints. Search scope included GridSFM, AC-OPF warm starting, OPF benchmarks, and LoRA/parameter-efficient power-flow adaptation through 21 October 2026. Future-dated 2027 issue records surfaced in search but were not used as already-published evidence.
